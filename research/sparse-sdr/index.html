Sparse Sufficient Dimension Reduction via Distance Covariance

A prospective research direction in high-dimensional statistics combining sufficient dimension reduction, nonlinear dependence, sparsity and optimisation on matrix manifolds. The project is currently being discussed rather than presented as formally commenced work.

IN DISCUSSION HIGH-DIMENSIONAL STATISTICS MANIFOLD OPTIMISATION SPARSITY
Status note. Project 11 appears in the University of Sydney 2026 Statistics Honours project guide with Dr Linh Nghiem and Dr Andi Han as the listed supervisors. My possible participation is still under discussion, so this page records the research direction and preparation without implying that supervision or an Honours candidature has been formally confirmed.

Research problem

Reduce dimension without throwing away the response-relevant structure

In sufficient dimension reduction (SDR), a high-dimensional predictor X is replaced by a small number of linear combinations while retaining the information needed to explain or predict a response Y. Rather than estimating a full regression surface in a large predictor space, the central task becomes estimating a lower-dimensional subspace.

Z = BᵀX,   with   B ∈ ℝp×d   and   d ≪ p

The official project description focuses on a recent SDR approach that maximises distance covariance between the response and the projected predictors, subject to a manifold constraint on B. Distance covariance is attractive here because it captures nonlinear dependence and can work beyond narrowly specified regression models.

Proposed extension

From a predictive subspace to a sparse, interpretable subspace

A projection can be statistically useful while still depending on many original variables. The proposed extension introduces additional structure—especially sparsity—so that the learned subspace can select or heavily prioritise a smaller set of variables while preserving the SDR objective.

1 · Dependence

Distance covariance

Use a general dependence measure to make the reduced representation retain information about the response.

2 · Structure

Sparse subspace estimation

Impose row- or variable-level structure so the projection is more parsimonious and potentially interpretable.

3 · Geometry

Manifold optimisation

Respect the subspace/orthogonality geometry of the parameter while developing computationally efficient algorithms.

Research questions

Questions I would want to investigate

How should sparsity be imposed?

Compare penalties or structured constraints that encourage variable selection without destroying the geometry of the SDR problem.

How should the structural dimension be chosen?

Study principled ways to select the effective dimension d rather than assuming it is known in advance.

How should optimisation respect the subspace?

Develop or compare algorithms on Stiefel/Grassmann-type geometries and examine their stability in high dimensions.

When does the method help in practice?

Evaluate statistical recovery, computational cost and predictive information on challenging high-dimensional datasets.

Why this direction matters to me

A bridge between statistics and optimisation

My earlier SCDL3991 work made optimisation geometry a concrete research problem: performance changed when the geometry of a score field became anisotropic even when the nominal problem size did not increase dramatically. Sparse SDR offers a different setting in which geometry again matters, but now the goal is statistical dimension reduction and variable structure rather than score learning. That makes the project a useful bridge between my statistics training and my developing interest in optimisation for machine learning.

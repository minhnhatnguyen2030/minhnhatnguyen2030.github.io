Geometry-Driven Benchmarking of Optimisers for Score Learning

A semester-long individual research project investigating how multimodal geometry influences optimisation in diffusion-inspired score learning.

High Distinction · 88/100 Individual Research PyTorch Score Learning Optimisation Gaussian Mixture Models
SCDL3991 unit ↗ Dr Andi Han ↗ Request full report

Research question

Diffusion models depend on accurate score estimation, but the optimisation problem is shaped by the geometry of the underlying probability distribution. I used Gaussian Mixture Models as a controlled setting in which the true score is analytically tractable, allowing optimiser behaviour to be studied without the ambiguity of an unknown target score.

Experimental design

I trained a common time-conditioned neural network to regress the exact score across four mixture configurations, multiple diffusion-noise levels and multiple sample sizes. Six optimisers were compared: SGD, Momentum, SignedGD, Adam, Muon and Muon without momentum.

Main finding

The most important result was not simply that Adam performed best. The asymmetric three-component mixture produced disproportionately large errors despite having fewer modes than some symmetric configurations. This supported the interpretation that curvature anisotropy and irregular responsibility boundaries, rather than mode count alone, were major drivers of optimisation difficulty.

Research lesson: nominal complexity is a poor proxy for learning difficulty. Understanding the structure and geometry of a learning problem can be more informative than simply counting components, parameters or data points.

What this project changed for me

This project shifted my attention from asking only whether an optimiser or model performs well toward asking why particular learning dynamics are robust or fragile. That question now informs my broader interest in reasoning, representation and the mechanisms through which capable AI behaviour emerges.

Project setting

Turn a domain question into a statistical problem

The Australian Health Survey project is deliberately interdisciplinary. Nutrition students contribute domain-led questions, while Statistics students help reformulate those questions into an analysable statistical problem, work with the data, and communicate the findings back to the broader team.

That structure makes the project different from a conventional individual modelling assignment: the quality of the work depends not only on selecting an algorithm but also on defining the estimand or prediction target correctly, understanding the data-generating context, checking assumptions and presenting conclusions in language that remains useful outside Statistics.

My role

Statistics / machine-learning lead

Within the group, I am leading the quantitative side of the work. My role is to keep the statistical question, modelling choices and evaluation logic coherent while supporting collaboration with teammates working from a nutrition/domain perspective.

Audit the dataInspect variables, coding, missingness, distributions, possible confounding and limitations of the survey data.
Formulate the statistical taskTranslate the domain question into a modelling or inference problem with explicit assumptions and evaluation criteria.

Validate and stress-testCheck robustness, diagnostics, uncertainty and whether conclusions remain stable under reasonable alternative choices.
CommunicateConvert the technical result into clear evidence, limitations and implications that an interdisciplinary audience can scrutinise.

STAT3888 toolkit

Methods available through the unit

STAT3888 covers a broad statistical-machine-learning toolkit. The items below describe the course methods, not a claim that every method will be used in our final project. The final analysis should be driven by the research question and the structure of the Australian Health Survey data.

Dimension reduction

Principal component analysis and related multivariate structure.

Classification

Discriminant methods, classification trees, k-nearest neighbours and logistic regression.

Unsupervised learning

K-means and hierarchical clustering for exploratory structure.

Graphical modelling

Gaussian graphical models for conditional-dependence structure.

Categorical modelling

Log-linear modelling and multivariate testing where appropriate.

Evaluation

Method comparison, validation and careful interpretation rather than algorithm selection alone.

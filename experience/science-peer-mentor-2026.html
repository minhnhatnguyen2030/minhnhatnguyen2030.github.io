Science Peer Mentor

A year-long peer leadership experience supporting commencing Science students as they adjust to university, find their community and learn how to navigate academic life at the University of Sydney.

Role & context

I serve as a Science Peer Mentor across 2026, helping first-year students make the transition into university life with practical guidance, regular check-ins and an approachable peer connection inside the Faculty of Science. The official program is designed around senior mentors helping commencing students meet peers, gain insight into university life and stay connected through online communication, catch-ups and Faculty activities.

For me, the role is less about having all the answers and more about creating a space where new students feel comfortable asking questions, sharing concerns and gradually becoming confident enough to navigate university independently.

Mentoring in practice

In Semester 1, I worked with co-mentors including Gabriela Michelle Vivaldi. Her public reflection on our mentoring experience describes six weeks of supporting a group of ten students through coffee chats, Welcome Day, STEM-Fest, pizza lunches, dodgeball and a large campus tour — a good snapshot of how the program mixes practical transition support with community-building.

What I developed

The experience strengthened my communication, group facilitation and leadership-through-service skills. It also reinforced a lesson that applies well beyond mentoring: strong academic environments are built not only through classes and research, but through people making time to help one another find their footing.

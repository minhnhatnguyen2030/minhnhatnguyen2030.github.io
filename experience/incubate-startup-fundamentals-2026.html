Why I joined

I joined INCUBATE's Startup Fundamentals program because technical capability alone does not create impact. I wanted structured exposure to the questions that come after an idea: What problem is actually worth solving? Who needs the solution? How should a value proposition be communicated? How do teams, networks and founder communities help an idea evolve?

In my introduction to the cohort, I described myself as a Statistics and Data Science student interested in innovation, startups and using analytical thinking to tackle meaningful problems, with particular curiosity about the intersection of data, AI and entrepreneurship.

The program

Startup Fundamentals is INCUBATE's introductory eight-week course for the University of Sydney community. The 2026 Semester 1 program ran from 18 March to 7 May through weekly online workshops covering startup frameworks, entrepreneurial mindset, networking, idea refinement, group tasks and founder meet-and-greets.

The sessions gave me a useful counterpoint to academic problem-solving: instead of focusing only on whether a method is technically strong, the startup lens asks whether the problem matters, whether people will adopt the solution and how an idea can be communicated clearly enough for collaborators, mentors or users to engage with it.

What I took from it

The program gave me a practical framework for thinking beyond model performance toward users, networks, communication and implementation. That perspective now informs how I think about AI research as well: sophisticated technology matters, but so do the human problem, the adoption context and the pathway from an idea to measurable impact.

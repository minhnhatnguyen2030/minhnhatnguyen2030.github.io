BUILD STORY 02

Could an AI system notice when a lecture loses you — and pull you back in?

At FoundersHack Sydney 2026 I led the AI integration workstream for LevelUp, a Chrome-based study companion designed to make passive online lectures more interactive. My focus was the intelligence layer: connect what the lecture is saying and showing to contextual reactions, questions and feedback in real time.

01

PROBLEM

Engagement is a systems problem, not just a chatbot prompt.

The product idea came from a familiar tutoring pattern: students can silently lose the thread long before they ask for help. LevelUp explored a different interaction model — an AI companion that observes the same lecture context as the learner, reacts to it, and creates a short challenge from the material that was actually presented.

02

AI LEAD · SYSTEM INTEGRATION

Ground the interaction in what the learner is actually seeing.

The stack combined a TypeScript browser extension with live transcription, periodic slide understanding and LLM generation. The public project used Deepgram for transcription, Claude Vision for slide context, Sonnet for contextual reactions and quiz generation, plus a Canvas game layer and generated voice output.

InputLive audio + lecture slides
PerceptionASR + visual context
Reasoning layerContextual LLM generation
InteractionReaction + lecture-grounded quiz

My role was to own the AI-integration stream inside a remote team: thinking through how these components should exchange context and how to turn separate model capabilities into one coherent learner-facing loop.

03

60-HOUR BUILD PRESSURE

Real products fail in places benchmark tables do not show.

The build surfaced constraints that are easy to ignore in an academic prototype: browser content-security policy, capture failures, expiring API identifiers, latency, and remote coordination. Those failures changed how I think about AI engineering. A model can be capable while the overall experience remains unreliable.

Shipping quickly was valuable because it exposed the gap between “the model works” and “the system works for a user.”
04

FOUNDERS · OPERATORS · VENTURE ECOSYSTEM

Learning how technical ideas are pressure-tested outside the lab.

FoundersHack deliberately emphasised customer discovery before building and placed the build phase inside Folklore Ventures. Alongside that sprint, I have sought startup training and ecosystem exposure through founder, operator and investor communities connected with organisations such as Folklore, Blackbird and Startmate.

Conversations with builders — including Ethan Trang ↗ — reinforced a useful discipline: be able to explain not only what the AI system can do, but why a user should care, how the system will be evaluated, and what would have to become reliable before it deserves adoption.

I describe these as founder / VC ecosystem exposure and training — not employment, investment, or formal affiliation with the venture firms named here.

FROM RESEARCH TO PRODUCT

Keep the research instinct, add product reality.

LevelUp gave me a compact version of the path I want to keep practising: understand a human problem, build a technically ambitious AI system, evaluate where the interaction fails, and iterate with feedback rather than treating the demo as the finish line.

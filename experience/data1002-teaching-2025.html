01

SEMESTER 2 · 2024

Before the title — DATA1002 as a first-year student

I took DATA1002: Informatics — Data and Computation in my first year at the University of Sydney. The unit introduced the computational foundations of data science: Python, data ingestion and cleaning, summarisation, automation of common data-science processes, and the creation and application of predictive models.

The part of the unit that ended up shaping me most, however, was not an assessment. It was the student discussion community on Ed.

Throughout the semester I became one of the most active students on the DATA1X02 discussion board — asking questions, answering other students, following discussions, and helping classmates work through material. By the final staff leaderboard, I ranked first in five of the six displayed engagement categories: 83 answers, 503 comments, 8,590 views, 870 hearts and 28 endorsements. I also asked 35 questions.

Before I had a teaching title, I was already discovering that explaining an idea to someone else was one of the best ways to understand it myself.
02

SEMESTER 1 · 2025

Advanced data, same habit — DATA2901

The following semester I progressed to DATA2901: Big Data and Data Diversity (Advanced). The unit moved beyond introductory computation into heterogeneous and larger-scale data: relational and semi-structured data, time series, geospatial information, images and text, together with Python, SQL, databases and the challenges of processing large data volumes.

As the material became more advanced, the same peer-learning habit continued. In the DATA2X01 Ed leaderboard shared by Davin Najanurdin ↗, I finished as the top answerer with 71 answers and top commenter with 318 comments.

What mattered to me was not the ranking itself. It showed that the behaviour from DATA1002 had survived contact with harder material: learn openly, ask questions, and help someone else when I could.

03

6 MAY · 2025

A recommendation changed the role

One of my DATA2901 tutors, Daniel Friedrich ↗, noticed that involvement. When I decided to apply for casual academic tutoring for the following semester, Daniel wrote a recommendation letter supporting my application.

The letter highlighted my willingness to support students at the DATA2001 helpdesk and on Ed, my programming ability, my experience with SQL and datasets, and my academic performance across DATA1001, DATA1002 and DATA2901. He also described my enthusiasm for teaching and learning as one of the reasons he believed I could contribute to a future teaching team.

His email was brief: “Please apply, I hope you get a position.” For me, that was the moment an informal contribution started becoming a formal teaching path.

04

PEOPLE · RESEARCH · TEACHING

Conversations beyond the classroom

My relationship with the DATA2X01 teaching community extended beyond coursework. I had many personal conversations with my lecturer Nasim Ahmed ↗ about academia, PhD study, research and teaching.

Those conversations helped me see research and teaching less as separate paths and more as activities that continually inform one another. Teaching forces an idea to become explainable; research forces you to test whether the idea was understood in the first place.

One discussion thread concerned how machine-learning approaches behave as problems change in scale, and how well a pipeline generalises beyond the setting in which it was initially evaluated. Related ideas later came up in conversations with Aishwarya Manivannan ↗ around her SCDL3991 project on the generalisability of a machine-learning pipeline for prediction tasks.

This is included as an example of academic conversation and exchange of ideas; it is not presented as a formal research contribution to Aishwarya's project.

05

TUTOR APPLICATION · 2025

The interview — returning to a former lecturer

My interview for the DATA1002 tutoring role was conducted by Josiah Poon ↗, who had previously taught me.

That made the interview feel like another transition point: I was no longer speaking to a lecturer only as one of his students, but applying to become part of the teaching team for a unit I had completed just a year earlier.

Josiah's research profile in machine learning, natural language processing, text mining and health informatics also represented the connection between computation, data science and research that I was increasingly interested in pursuing.

06

SEMESTER 2 · 2025

Returning to DATA1002 — this time as a tutor

In Semester 2, 2025, I returned to DATA1002 as a tutor. Only one year separated the first-year student asking and answering questions on Ed from the tutor helping a new cohort work through the same foundations of computational data science.

The role formalised something I had already been practising through peer learning: helping students navigate Python, data handling and the reasoning behind a data-science workflow rather than treating a technical problem as only a piece of code to be completed.

The most meaningful part of the transition was remembering how recently I had occupied the same position as the students I was now helping. I still remembered which ideas initially felt unintuitive, what it was like to get stuck on a programming problem, and how valuable a patient explanation from a tutor or another student could be.

07

SEMESTER 2 · 2025

Still a student — DATA2902

Becoming a tutor did not mean I stopped being a learner. During the same semester, I took the advanced DATA2902 pathway and continued contributing to the shared DATA2X02 academic community.

In the final wrap-up posted by lecturer Shila Ghazanfar, I was listed second among the top question answerers. The post displayed my name as “Minh” — a shortened rendering people sometimes used for Minh-Nhat.

The staff summary also showed the scale of that learning community: roughly 1,200 students participated on Ed, with around 430,000 views, 1,200 threads, 1,300 answers and 2,600 comments across the semester. Even while formally teaching one data-science cohort, I was still learning alongside and helping another.

WHAT I CARRY FORWARD

Learner → contributor → teacher → learner again.

DATA1002, DATA2901 and DATA2902 taught me increasingly sophisticated technical material, but they also showed me what an academic community can look like when students actively help one another.

The recommendation from Daniel, conversations with Nasim, collaboration with tutors such as Davin, the interview with Josiah, research conversations with peers such as Aishwarya, and the Ed communities themselves all became parts of the same story.

It is one reason I now see research and teaching as closely connected: both begin with curiosity, both depend on being willing to ask questions, and both become more meaningful when knowledge is shared rather than kept to oneself.

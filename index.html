One year separated two very different datathons. In 2024, Calvin and I entered a stock-prediction challenge and did not place. In 2025, we returned as part of Team Clesmor, changed our modelling strategy when prediction failed, and won 1st place. This is the story of that progression.

🏆 1st Place · 2025 Supply-chain optimisation Forecasting · ML · Gurobi 24-hour datathon
Chapter I · 2024

First attempt: predict the market.

The 2024 SUDATA × SUBAA Datathon was centred on financial prediction. Our project analysed historical JP Morgan stock prices and incorporated tweet sentiment as a predictive feature, combining time-series analysis, preprocessing, machine learning and cross-validation.

We did not place that year. What remained, however, was experience: building under a hard deadline, deciding which signals were worth trusting, and seeing how quickly a technically plausible predictive story can become fragile when it meets real data.

In the 2024 group photo below, Calvin and I are the two men standing at the far left. A year later, we would return to the same datathon and finish in first place.

Chapter II · 2025

We came back with a different kind of problem.

The 2025 challenge moved from financial prediction to supply-chain management. Team Clesmor — Lyra, Carmen, Calvin, Shane and Thomas — had 24 hours to turn raw logistics data into an actionable recommendation.

The important part was not that our first model worked. It did not. Shipment-level prediction showed little useful signal. Rather than spending the remaining time forcing a weak predictive model to look convincing, we changed the question.

Instead of asking “Can we accurately predict every shipment?”, we moved toward “Can the information we do trust support a better logistics decision?”

What changed

From predictive modelling to prescriptive optimisation.

The 2024 entry was predominantly a predictive-modelling exercise: historical prices, sentiment features and machine-learning models. In 2025, the technical scope became broader. We still investigated predictive models and forecasting, but the decisive step was recognising when those models were not informative enough and pivoting toward optimisation.

Data ingestion→ EDA & feature engineering→ Prediction / forecasting→ Model failure diagnosis→ Network construction→ Gurobi optimisation→ Business recommendation

Forecasting

Time-series work included ETS and hierarchical forecast reconciliation, exploring whether delivery patterns could support operational planning.

Machine Learning

Cost and delay models were tested rather than assumed to be useful. Weak held-out performance became evidence for changing the problem formulation.

Optimisation

A data-derived supply-chain network was optimised across cost, delivery reliability and emissions, turning analysis into an interpretable decision problem.

The most important lesson from the 2025 project was therefore methodological: a failed model is still useful if it tells you to ask a better question. That pivot — from prediction to decision-making — became the centre of our winning submission.

Outcome

1st Place — SUDATA × SUBAA Datathon 2025.

Team Clesmor was awarded first place in the 2025 competition. For me, the certificate represents more than the final ranking: it connects the 2024 attempt with a year of accumulated modelling, statistical and collaborative experience.

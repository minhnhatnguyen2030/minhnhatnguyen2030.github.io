My AI Journey · Story 02

Summer 2026 in Seoul

Inspiration, friendship, and the journey that made Korea my next destination.

Seoul & Daejeon, South Korea · June–August 2026 · University of Sydney

01

An Open Page

Five years after a summer in Saigon first pushed me to think seriously about how technology and artificial intelligence might shape my future, I arrived in Seoul as an undergraduate from the University of Sydney. By then, AI had become part of my academic life through statistics, machine learning, research, and code. Yet much of the future I imagined still existed on paper or behind a screen.

I came to Korea expecting six weeks of study, research events, and exploration. Instead, Seoul became a turning point through an accumulation of ordinary days: unfamiliar streets, long classes, unexpected conversations, mistakes, music, rain, and people I was fortunate enough to meet along the way.

I called the collection my Seoul Diary. Looking back, perhaps the first lesson was simply to leave some pages unwritten and remain open to what could fill them.

02

Seoul as a Classroom

At Yonsei, my days quickly found a rhythm of classes, assignments, coffee, and late evenings walking through Sinchon. Studying beyond my usual technical subjects reminded me that understanding intelligence requires more than understanding algorithms. Human behaviour, culture, communication, and society increasingly sat beside statistics and machine learning in the way I thought about technology.

I also became fascinated by Korea's 빨리빨리 — ppalli-ppalli — culture. Seoul seemed constantly in motion. At first I found the pace exhausting; later I began to appreciate the willingness to move quickly while staying open to new ideas, new people, and new possibilities.

But summer rain taught me the opposite lesson. Wet paths forced everyone to slow down. So did learning pottery: rushing the clay only made the form collapse.

Seoul taught me to accelerate. Rain and clay taught me when to slow down.

03

Inspiration Beyond AI

Some of the strongest inspiration I found in Korea had little to do with machine learning. There were books and writers, museums and pottery, baseball games, K-pop concerts, long walks, and ordinary evenings that gradually made Seoul feel less like somewhere I was merely visiting.

Music became especially important. If research expanded the questions I wanted to answer, music and culture expanded the kind of life I wanted those answers to belong to. I began to see that pursuing AI seriously did not have to mean reducing life to optimisation, publications, or career milestones. The future I wanted to help build still needed room for creativity, relationships, curiosity, and the parts of being human that are difficult to quantify.

04

When the Future Became Real

Then came ICML 2026. For several days, the field I had mostly encountered through papers, lectures, and screens became physical. Thousands of researchers filled the halls of COEX. I moved between talks, poster sessions, industry booths, and conversations on topics ranging from familiar statistical ideas to research far beyond my current understanding.

What affected me most was not simply the technical sophistication. It was seeing the people behind it. Researchers I had imagined as distant figures were carrying backpacks, queueing for coffee, sitting on the floor between sessions, laughing with friends, debating ideas, and becoming tired like everyone else. The frontier suddenly looked less like an unreachable world and more like a community of ordinary people doing extraordinarily difficult work.

For the first time, I could genuinely imagine myself trying to become part of that community. The question was no longer only what will AI become? It was also who do I want to become around it?

05

People Along the Way

ICML gave me something no paper could: people. Some conversations lasted only a few minutes. Others became friendships. Meeting researchers and students from different countries showed me how many paths could lead into AI, and how much of a research life is built not only through ideas but through the people with whom those ideas are shared.

One friendship in particular became unexpectedly meaningful. At a time when I was both excited and slightly intimidated by how quickly AI was advancing, being able to talk openly about research, work, happiness, and ordinary life reminded me that professional ambition does not need to consume everything around it.

I had arrived looking for people who could teach me about AI. Instead, some of the most important lessons were about life. A friend reminded me, simply through the way they lived, that a genuinely happy life might be less complicated than I had imagined.

06

From Seoul to Daejeon

Near the end of the summer, after finishing my assessments at Yonsei, I travelled south through the rain to Daejeon. Until then, Korea had been a collection of experiences: Seoul, Yonsei, ICML, research, friendship, concerts, books, rain, and hundreds of small memories.

Visiting KAIST turned those experiences into a more concrete question: could I imagine building the next stage of my life here?

07

Why KAIST Became the Next Stop

Walking through KAIST, meeting students, and seeing the environment in which research was being done made the possibility of returning to Korea feel real. Korea was no longer simply somewhere I enjoyed visiting, and graduate study there was no longer an abstract idea. I could imagine returning not for another summer, but as a researcher.

KAIST represented many of the things that had drawn me toward Korea during the summer: serious scientific ambition, an energetic AI ecosystem, close connections between research and technology, and a culture willing to move quickly toward the future. I even spent part of my birthday there with people I had only recently met — an ordinary celebration that made the possibility of returning feel unexpectedly personal.

By the time I left Daejeon, I had reached a decision. I did not know whether Korea would choose me back. Admission, research opportunities, and the future itself remained uncertain. But I knew where I wanted to try next.

KAIST had become the next stop I wanted to pursue — not because one visit answered every question, but because the summer had finally given my questions a direction.

08

More Than a University

Looking back, I do not think I chose KAIST because of a single building, ranking, research group, or conference. The decision had been forming throughout the summer — in classrooms at Yonsei, conversations at ICML, Seoul's speed and its rain, researchers who made the frontier feel reachable, and friends who made Korea feel less foreign.

KAIST gave that collection of experiences a direction. Seoul gave the direction meaning.

09

Way Back Home

Toward the end of the summer, beneath the lights of Seoul, I made a simple wish: 모두, 그리고 저도 언제나 행복하기를. May everyone — including me — always be happy.

It was a different ambition from the ones I had arrived with. I still wanted to do serious work and pursue AI research, but Seoul had quietly changed my definition of a successful future. I wanted the work, but also the people, curiosity, friendship, music, ordinary happiness, and sense of being alive that made the work worth doing.

10

A New Chapter

When the time came to leave, I wrote that my Seoul Diary had come to an end. For someone stubborn enough to joke that changing my perspective would require brain surgery, the summer somehow managed to do exactly that — quietly, through ordinary days, conversations, and the people I met along the way.

Researchers showed me that the people building extraordinary things were still ordinary human beings. Seoul taught me to move quickly. Rain taught me to slow down. AI made the future fascinating. Friendship made the future worth reaching.

In Korean, I wrote: 서울에서 보낸 여름은 나를 많이 변화시켰고, 이제 나는 인생의 새로운 장을 시작합니다.

The summer I spent in Seoul changed me deeply, and now I am beginning a new chapter of my life.

I still do not know exactly where that chapter will lead. What changed in 2026 was not that the future became certain. It was that I found a direction worth pursuing.

The Seoul Diary ended. The journey did not.

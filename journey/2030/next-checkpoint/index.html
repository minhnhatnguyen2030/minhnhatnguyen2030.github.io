2030: To Be Continued

I do not know yet where I will be, what I will be working on, or which questions will matter most. I only hope that, if life allows, I will still know when to slow down.

2030 · Location unknown · Story unwritten

?

If life allows, slow down.

By 2030, I may be deep in research, building systems, chasing deadlines, or living a life that I cannot imagine from 2026. The field I want to enter rewards speed. There will always be another paper, model, experiment, application, message, or milestone asking to be next.

Haemin Sunim's The Things You Can See Only When You Slow Down leaves me with a different kind of checkpoint. The state of the mind changes the way the world is experienced. When everything inside feels urgent, the world can begin to look urgent too. A quieter mind can reveal details that speed makes invisible.

So this page is not a prediction of 2030. It is a note to my future self: keep the ambition, but do not let ambition erase the people, places, health, silence, curiosity, music, friendships, and ordinary days that gave the ambition meaning in the first place.

If life allows, slow down — not to stop moving forward, but to notice what you are moving through.

Take the walk. Call home. Stay for the conversation. Listen to the song without multitasking. Let a difficult problem rest overnight. Look out the window. Some questions become clearer when they are not being forced.

2030 NOTE

Do not confuse acceleration with direction.

Moving quickly can be useful. Seoul taught me that. But the same summer also taught me the opposite lesson through rain, pottery, long walks, and people who made an ambitious future feel more human. I want both lessons to survive.

If 2030 is busy, I hope this page interrupts it for a minute. If 2030 is difficult, I hope it makes room for patience. And if 2030 is going well, I hope I remember to actually experience it rather than immediately turning it into the next target.

TO BE CONTINUED

The future can stay unwritten.

Wherever 2030 finds me, I hope I have not mistaken constant motion for a meaningful life. Build seriously. Care deeply. Keep going. And when life gives room, slow down enough to see where you are.

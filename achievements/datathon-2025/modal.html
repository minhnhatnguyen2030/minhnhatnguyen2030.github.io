From the Far Left
to First Place.

In 2024, Calvin and I entered the SUDATA × SUBAA Datathon and did not place. One year later, we returned as part of Team Clesmor and won 1st place.

🏆 1st Place · 2025 Team Clesmor 24-hour Datathon Forecasting × ML × Optimisation
2024

The first attempt

Prediction first.

The 2024 competition centred on predicting JP Morgan stock-price movements. Our project was predominantly a machine-learning and predictive-modelling exercise: historical price time series, tweet-sentiment features, preprocessing, model training and cross-validation.

We did not make the podium. But it became our first full Datathon cycle: formulate, build, test, present, fail, and learn under severe time pressure.

ONE YEAR LATER
2025

The return

A better question changed the competition.

The 2025 challenge moved from financial prediction to supply-chain management. Team Clesmor — Lyra, Carmen, Calvin, Shane and Thomas — had 24 hours to transform raw logistics data into a defensible operational recommendation.

The decisive pivot

Our initial predictive models underperformed. Rather than force a weak model into the final story, we changed the problem from individual prediction to decision-making.

Initial question Can we accurately predict every shipment?
→
Better question Can the information we trust support a better logistics decision?
Data→ EDA→ Prediction→ Pivot→ Forecasting→ Network optimisation→ Decision
01

Forecasting

ETS and hierarchical forecast reconciliation explored delivery patterns and operational planning at aggregated levels.

02

Machine Learning

Weak held-out predictive performance was treated as evidence rather than hidden, motivating a change in problem formulation.

03

Optimisation

A data-derived supply-chain network was optimised across cost, delivery reliability and emissions to support decision-making.

A failed model can still be valuable when it tells you to ask a better question.

Outcome

1st Place — 2025.

Team Clesmor won the SUDATA × SUBAA Datathon 2025. For Thomas, the award links two moments: standing at the far left of the 2024 group photo after an unsuccessful first attempt, and returning one year later with a stronger team, a broader technical toolkit, and a more mature way of framing data problems.
